# Style: museum exhibit tour

A piece that plays like a nighttime museum special-exhibit walkthrough: a single visitor moves room to room through a pitch-dark gallery where each "diorama" is picked out by one hanging spotlight, a low-poly artifact on a pedestal, a cream museum wall-label, and a docent's caption bar typing out commentary along the bottom. The overall feeling is hushed, reverent, and slightly theatrical — closer to an after-hours exhibit tour than a tech product demo, with warm sepia light carving objects out of near-total black.

## Direction
Render a nighttime "special exhibit" walkthrough: a sequence of static, locked-off wide shots, each one a pitch-black gallery room lit by exactly one warm sepia spotlight cone (with faint drifting dust motes) that carves a single low-poly faceted object — flat-shaded, hard facet edges, rotating slowly — out of the darkness on a plain museum pedestal. Beside or below it, place a cream/parchment museum wall-label card: small eyebrow line, serif headline, thin rule, sans-serif detail lines, tiny source credit at the bottom. Whenever you must show a number or comparison, don't draw a normal chart — stage it as a museum artifact instead (a hanging price-tag with the old value struck through and the new one below, or a set of pedestal "pillars" of different heights each capped with its own placard, growing upward as the value counts up). Reserve one warm coral/terracotta accent color for whatever should read as "the highlight" (an active tag, the winning pillar, the current step marker) and keep everything else in neutral grays, warm blacks, and dim gold. Keep a fixed small-caps location header in one corner and a room-trail progress dial (dots joined by short dashes, current one glowing) in the other. Along the bottom, run a full-width dark pill-shaped caption bar with a small rounded "guide" badge and narration text that types itself out. Move between rooms with a slow lateral pan past a dark dividing wall — glimpsing the edge of the last room and the next — never a hard cut, and let the spotlight fade to black and "search" back on rather than snapping instantly. Keep the soundtrack sparse: a quiet, continuous low ambient drone under everything, a soft high tick/click on most transitions, an occasional short bright chime accenting a reveal, and reserve one longer broadband swell for your single biggest "reveal" moment. Open and close on the same gold-triangle-spotlight title-card composition so the piece reads as a matched entrance and exit.
